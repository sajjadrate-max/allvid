Free up space on your phone for videos

Published October 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Videos are some of the largest files on any phone, so a "storage full" warning often shows up just when you want to save something important. The good news is that most phones have plenty of space hiding in places you do not use. Here is how to find it and keep it free.

Step 1: See what is using your space

Before deleting anything, take a look at where the space is going.

  • Android: open Settings, then Storage (on some phones it is under Battery and device care or Device care). You will see categories such as Apps, Images, Videos and Audio, and often a "Clean up" or "Free up space" suggestion.
  • iPhone: open Settings, General, iPhone Storage. You will see a list of apps sorted by size and suggestions such as offloading unused apps.

Most people find that a few apps, their photo and video library, and old downloads take up the majority of the space.

Step 2: Clear old downloads

Downloads folders quietly fill up with files you needed once: PDFs, installers, videos you have already watched. Open the Files app (Files by Google or your phone's file manager on Android, or the Files app on iPhone), go to Downloads and sort by size. Delete the large files you no longer need.

If you use AllVid, open My files. Each file shows the time it was downloaded, which makes it easy to spot old ones. Remove what you are done with. To clear everything at once, use the option to delete all files in AllVid's Settings.

Step 3: Tidy your photo and video library

  • Delete duplicates and near-duplicates. Burst shots and five versions of the same photo add up quickly. Many gallery apps can find duplicates for you.
  • Remove screen recordings and long accidental videos. A forgotten ten-minute recording of your pocket can take hundreds of megabytes.
  • Empty the "Recently deleted" folder. Deleted photos and videos usually stay there for about 30 days and still take up space until you empty it.
  • Back up, then remove. Copy videos you want to keep to a computer, an external drive or a cloud service, then delete them from the phone.

Step 4: Deal with large apps

Some apps grow very large over time because they store cached videos, images and offline content.

  • Clear the cache (Android): Settings, Apps, choose an app, Storage, Clear cache. This removes temporary files without deleting your account or settings. Avoid "Clear storage" or "Clear data" unless you are happy to reset that app.
  • Offload or uninstall unused apps: games and apps you have not opened in months are prime candidates. On iPhone, offloading keeps your data and removes the app itself.
  • Check messaging apps: chat apps often keep every video and photo anyone has ever sent you. Look for a "Manage storage" option inside the app to review and delete large media.
  • Remove offline content: downloaded music, podcasts, maps and streaming episodes can use gigabytes. Delete what you have already finished.

Step 5: Save smarter going forward

Once you have made room, a few habits will keep it that way.

  • Pick a sensible size. For watching on a phone, 720p usually looks great and uses far less space than 1080p. Save 1080p for keepsakes. See 360p vs 720p vs 1080p.
  • Save audio only when that is all you need. A talk or lesson as MP3 or M4A is a fraction of the size of the video. See MP3 vs M4A explained.
  • Set a preferred quality. In AllVid's Settings, choose your usual quality so you do not accidentally save huge files.
  • Do a monthly clean-up. Spend five minutes removing files you have watched.

Use a memory card if your phone has one

Many Android phones accept a microSD card. You can often set your camera or file manager to store new files on the card, or move existing videos there with the Files app. Choose a card from a well-known brand with a good speed rating, and remember that cards can fail, so keep backups of anything important.

How much space should you keep free?

Phones work best with some breathing room. Try to keep at least 10 to 15 percent of your storage free, or a few gigabytes, whichever is larger. Very full phones can become slow, struggle to install updates, and may stop downloads partway through. If a download in AllVid fails, low storage is one of the first things to check.

What not to delete

Be careful with system folders you do not recognise, especially on Android file managers that show everything. Stick to your own files, app caches and media. If you are unsure what something is, leave it alone. And before clearing anything that contains memories, make sure you have a copy somewhere else.
