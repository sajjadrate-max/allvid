360p vs 720p vs 1080p: which size should you choose?

Published October 25, 2026 · 3 min read

When you save a video, you are often offered several sizes: 360p, 720p, 1080p and sometimes more. Bigger sounds better, but it is not always the right choice. Here is what the numbers mean and how to choose.

What the numbers mean

The number is the height of the video in pixels, the tiny dots that make up the picture. The "p" stands for progressive, which simply means each frame is drawn in full. For a standard wide video:

SizePixels (wide video)Common name
360p640 × 360Standard definition
720p1280 × 720HD
1080p1920 × 1080Full HD

For vertical videos, such as most short-form clips, the numbers are simply turned on their side: a 1080p vertical video is about 1080 pixels wide and 1920 tall. Each step up holds more detail. A 1080p frame has more than twice as many pixels as 720p, and about nine times as many as 360p.

How size affects storage and data

More pixels usually means a bigger file. The exact size depends on the length of the video, how much movement there is, and how the platform compressed it, but as a rough guide for one minute of typical video:

  • 360p: often around 3 to 8 MB
  • 720p: often around 10 to 25 MB
  • 1080p: often around 20 to 50 MB or more

Those numbers add up. A ten-minute video at 1080p can easily take several hundred megabytes, which matters if you are on a limited mobile data plan or your phone is nearly full. A fast-moving video like sports or a dance clip usually produces a larger file than a talking head at the same resolution.

Match the size to your screen

The best size depends a lot on where you will watch.

  • Phone screens: Most phones are small enough that 720p looks clear and sharp to most people. 1080p is a little crisper, especially on larger phones, but the difference is often hard to notice at arm's length.
  • Tablets and laptops: Here 1080p starts to make a real difference, particularly for text, faces and fine details.
  • TVs: If you plan to cast or play the file on a television, choose the highest size available.
  • Tiny previews or audio-focused clips: If the picture does not matter much, such as a lecture or a podcast with a static image, 360p or even audio only is perfectly fine.

A quick decision guide

  • Choose 360p when you are on mobile data, your storage is nearly full, or you only need to see what happens rather than every detail.
  • Choose 720p as an everyday default for watching on a phone. It is the best balance of quality and size for most people.
  • Choose 1080p for keepsakes, backups of your own videos, or anything you will watch on a large screen.

Why a higher number is not always sharper

A saved file can never be better than the version the platform stores. If someone uploaded a blurry 480p video, a "1080p" option may just be the same blurry picture stretched larger, with a bigger file to match. Platforms also compress videos to save bandwidth, so a 1080p file from a social site will not look as good as the original from the camera. If a higher size does not look noticeably better, the smaller one will save you space.

What about the ⚡ sizes?

In AllVid, sizes marked with ⚡ are ready-made files that start downloading immediately. Other sizes may need a short "getting ready" step while the file is prepared. If you are in a hurry, a ⚡ size is the quickest choice, even if it is not the largest.

Frame rate and other details

You may sometimes see numbers like 30 or 60 after the size. That is the frame rate, the number of pictures shown per second. Sixty looks smoother for fast motion such as games or sports, but makes the file bigger. For most everyday clips, 30 is plenty.

The bottom line

There is no single right answer. For most people watching on a phone, 720p is the sweet spot. Go up to 1080p for anything you want to keep long-term or watch on a big screen, and drop to 360p when data or space is tight. If you only want the sound, see MP3 vs M4A explained.
