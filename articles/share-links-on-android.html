Using the Share button and copying links on Android

Published October 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Almost everything you see in a social app has its own web address, or link. Copying that link is the first step to saving your own videos, sending a post to a friend, or opening it in another app. Here is how to find it on Android, and how the Share button can save you a few taps.

What a link is

A link (also called a URL) is the address of a page or post, such as one beginning with https://. When you paste a link into a browser or a tool like AllVid, it points directly to that post. Links to public posts can be opened by anyone; links to private posts only work for people who are allowed to see them.

The Share button

Most apps put a Share button on every post. It often looks like an arrow, a paper plane, or three connected dots. Tapping it opens a panel with options. Inside apps, this panel is usually the app's own menu, with choices like Copy link, Send in a message, or Share to other apps. Choosing "Share to" or "More" opens Android's own Share sheet, which lists the apps on your phone that can receive the link.

Copying links in popular apps

Menus change from time to time, but these are the usual places to look:

  • TikTok: tap the Share arrow on the right side of a video, then Copy link.
  • Instagram: tap the paper-plane icon under a post or reel, then Copy link. You may also find it in the three-dot menu.
  • Facebook: tap Share under the post, then Copy link. On some videos the option is under the three-dot menu.
  • X: tap the Share icon under the post, then Copy link.
  • Snapchat: on a public Story or Spotlight, use the three-dot menu or the Share option, then Copy link. Private snaps do not have shareable links.
  • Pinterest: open the Pin, tap the Share icon, then Copy link.
  • YouTube: tap Share below the video, then Copy link.

After copying, many phones show a small "Copied" message or a preview of the link at the bottom of the screen.

Where copied links go: the clipboard

When you copy a link, Android places it on the clipboard, a temporary holding spot. To paste it, tap and hold inside a text box until the Paste option appears, then tap it. Many keyboards, including Gboard and Samsung Keyboard, also have a clipboard button that shows your recent copies, which is useful if you copied several links in a row.

Keep in mind that the clipboard usually holds only the most recent item, and recent Android versions may clear it after a while for privacy. If paste does not work, go back and copy the link again.

Sharing straight to AllVid

If you have installed the AllVid Android app, you can skip the copy and paste step:

  1. In the app with your video, tap Share.
  2. Choose Share to, More or Other apps to open Android's Share sheet.
  3. Pick AllVid from the list. The link opens in AllVid, ready for you to choose a format and size.

If you do not see AllVid in the list, scroll to the end or tap "More". Many phones let you pin apps you use often to the top of the Share sheet by long-pressing them. You can get the app from the "Get the AllVid Android app" button on the home page.

Using AllVid in the browser instead

Do not want to install anything? Copy the link as described above, open allvid.org in Chrome or another browser, tap the link box and paste. Everything else works the same way. Just keep the browser tab open until your download finishes.

Tips when a link does not work

  • Make sure the post is public. Links to private accounts, close-friends stories and direct messages cannot be opened by outside tools.
  • Copy the full link. If you copied text from a caption, you may have only part of the address. Use the app's Copy link button instead.
  • Remove extra text. Some apps share a sentence plus the link, such as "Check out this video! https://...". If a pasted link is not recognised, delete the extra words and keep only the address.
  • Try again later. Sites sometimes slow down or change things, and a link that fails now may work in a few minutes.

Sharing responsibly

Being able to copy a link does not mean you are allowed to download what it points to. Use these steps for your own videos and for content the creator has permitted you to keep. When in doubt, sharing the link itself, rather than a downloaded copy, is a good way to send something to friends while giving the creator credit and views. See saving videos responsibly for more.
