Back up your videos to a computer or cloud storage

Published October 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Phones get lost, dropped in water, stolen or reset. When that happens, any video stored only on the phone goes with it. A backup means keeping at least one more copy somewhere else. It does not need to be complicated, and once it is set up it takes only a few minutes a month.

The 3-2-1 idea, made simple

Professionals often talk about the "3-2-1" rule: keep three copies of important files, on two different kinds of storage, with one copy in a different place. For everyday use, a simpler version works well:

  • the copy on your phone;
  • a copy on a computer or external drive; and
  • ideally, a copy in cloud storage.

Even one extra copy is far better than none.

Option 1: Copy to a computer with a cable

This is free, fast and does not need an internet connection.

Android to Windows

  1. Connect your phone with a USB cable.
  2. On the phone, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer (sometimes called MTP).
  3. On the computer, open File Explorer, click your phone, then open Internal storage and the Download folder (or DCIM for camera videos).
  4. Copy the videos you want into a folder on your computer.

Android to Mac

Macs do not read Android phones directly over USB in the same way. You can use a file transfer app for Mac, or use one of the cloud or wireless options below.

iPhone to computer

On a Mac, connect the iPhone and use the Photos app or Finder. On Windows, the Photos app or File Explorer can import camera videos once you tap "Trust" on the iPhone. Files saved in the Files app can also be shared with AirDrop to a Mac.

Option 2: Use an external drive or memory stick

Many phones can connect directly to a USB drive using a USB-C or Lightning adapter, or a dual-ended flash drive. Open your Files app, select the videos and copy them to the drive. Store the drive somewhere safe, away from your phone. External drives are inexpensive, but they can fail too, so do not rely on one drive forever.

Option 3: Cloud storage

Cloud storage keeps a copy of your files on a provider's servers, so they survive even if every device in your home is lost. Common options include services built into your phone, such as Google Drive or Google Photos on Android and iCloud on iPhone, as well as other well-known providers.

  • Automatic backup: photo apps can back up your camera roll automatically. Check whether downloaded videos are included; some apps only back up the camera folder unless you also choose other folders.
  • Manual upload: open your cloud storage app and upload files or a whole folder.
  • Storage limits: free plans have limited space, and videos fill it quickly. Paid plans add more.
  • Upload on Wi-Fi: set backups to happen over Wi-Fi to avoid using mobile data.

Choose a reputable provider, use a strong password and turn on two-step verification, since your cloud account will hold personal memories.

What should you back up?

Focus on what you cannot get back: your own recordings, family videos, and finished edits of things you have posted. Files you can easily find again do not need the same care. If you have saved copies of your own social media posts, our guide on backing up your own videos explains how to get them onto your phone first.

Keep your backup tidy

A pile of unnamed files is hard to use years later. Before backing up, sort your keepers into folders and give them clear names, such as "2026-06-14 beach day.mp4". Our guide to organising your Downloads folder has a simple system. Use the same folder structure on your phone, computer and cloud so everything is easy to find.

Check that your backup works

A backup is only useful if you can open it. Every few months, pick a couple of files from your computer or cloud copy and play them. Make sure they open and play to the end.

A routine that sticks

  1. Pick a regular day, such as the first Sunday of each month.
  2. Move new keepers into your "keep" folder and rename them.
  3. Copy that folder to your computer or drive, and make sure it is uploading to the cloud.
  4. Once you have confirmed the copies, delete files you no longer need on the phone to free space.
