How to back up your own social media videos to your phone

Published October 25, 2026 · 4 min read

If you post videos on social media, the platform is probably the only place some of them exist. That works fine until an account gets locked, a post is removed by mistake, or an old phone with the original clip is wiped. A simple backup routine means your memories and your work stay with you.

Why your own videos need a backup

Most of us assume that once a video is uploaded, it is safe forever. In reality, a lot can go wrong:

  • Original files disappear. Many people record and edit inside an app, so the only finished version is the uploaded one. Drafts get deleted, and old phones get reset.
  • Accounts change. You might lose access to an account, close it, or have it suspended while a problem is sorted out.
  • Platforms change. Services shut down features, change rules or remove older content. A post that is there today is not guaranteed to be there in ten years.
  • You want it offline. Showing a clip to family on a plane or in a place with weak signal is much easier when the file is on your phone.

Start with the official export tools

Several major platforms offer a way to download a copy of your own data, often found in account or privacy settings under names like "Download your information" or "Request your data". These exports can include your posts, captions and messages. They are a good choice for a complete archive, although they can take a while to arrive and may come as a large archive file that is awkward to browse on a phone.

For individual videos you want quick access to, saving them one by one to your phone is often more practical. Some apps also let you save your own posts directly from the post menu, sometimes with a watermark added.

Saving individual videos with AllVid

AllVid is designed for exactly this: getting a copy of a video you own onto your phone without an account or extra software.

  1. Open the app where you posted the video and find the post.
  2. Tap Share, then Copy link. On Android you can also share the link straight to the AllVid app.
  3. Open allvid.org or the AllVid app and paste the link.
  4. Choose a format and size. For a keepsake, pick the highest quality offered; for quick sharing, a smaller size is fine.
  5. Wait for the file to save. It will appear in My files with the time you downloaded it.

If you have a lot of videos to save, the Android app is worth installing. It passes each download to your phone's download manager, so large files keep downloading even if you switch apps or the screen turns off. In Settings you can choose how many downloads run at once.

Choose the right quality for a backup

A backup is about keeping the best version you can. If 1080p is offered, it will look sharpest on a big screen later. If storage is tight, 720p is a good compromise that still looks clear on phones. Our guide to 360p vs 720p vs 1080p explains the trade-offs. Keep in mind that the saved file can only be as good as the version the platform stores; most platforms compress uploads, so the original file from your camera is always best if you still have it.

Organise as you go

A pile of files called "video (12).mp4" is hard to use later. A few habits help:

  • Rename important files with the date and a short description, for example "2026-06-14 beach day".
  • Use folders by year or by topic in your phone's Files app.
  • Check My files after a session of saving; the download time makes it easy to see what you just added.

Keep more than one copy

A file that only exists on your phone is still at risk if the phone is lost or broken. For videos that really matter, copy them to a second place: a computer, an external drive, or a cloud storage service you trust. A simple rule many people use is to keep at least two copies, in two different places.

What counts as "your own" video?

This routine is for content you created and posted yourself. Videos where you appear but someone else filmed and posted them belong to the person who made them, so ask before saving. The same goes for clips friends send you: if they say you can keep them, go ahead. Our article on saving videos responsibly covers the basics.

A simple routine

Once a month, take ten minutes to save anything new you have posted that you would hate to lose, rename the files, and copy them to a second location. It is a small habit that can save years of memories.
