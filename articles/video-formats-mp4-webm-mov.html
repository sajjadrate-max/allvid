Video file formats explained: MP4, WebM and MOV

Published October 25, 2026 · 4 min read

The letters at the end of a video file name, such as .mp4, .webm or .mov, tell your phone what kind of file it is. They matter mostly for one reason: whether the file will play on the device you want to use. Here is a plain-language guide to the three formats you are most likely to meet.

Containers and codecs: the box and what is inside

A video file is a bit like a box. The box is called the container, and the file extension usually names it: MP4, WebM, MOV. Inside the box are the actual video and sound, each compressed with a method called a codec. The box can also hold extras such as subtitles, a thumbnail or information about the file.

This is why two MP4 files can behave differently. One may contain video compressed with H.264, which nearly everything can play, while another contains a newer codec such as H.265 (also called HEVC) or AV1, which older devices may struggle with. The container tells you a lot, but not everything.

MP4: the everyday standard

MP4 (MPEG-4 Part 14) is the most widely used video format in the world. Most MP4 files contain H.264 video and AAC audio, a combination that plays on virtually every phone, tablet, computer, smart TV and web browser made in the last decade or more.

  • Strengths: plays almost everywhere, good quality for its size, easy to share and edit.
  • Weaknesses: files using newer codecs inside an MP4 box may not play on older hardware.
  • Best for: keeping videos on your phone, sending to friends, and anything where you want the fewest surprises.

This is why AllVid offers video as MP4: it is the format most likely to just work when you tap play.

WebM: built for the web

WebM is an open, royalty-free format developed for web video. It usually contains video compressed with VP8, VP9 or AV1, and audio compressed with Vorbis or Opus. Many websites use it behind the scenes to stream video efficiently, especially at higher resolutions.

  • Strengths: efficient compression, open standard, excellent support in modern browsers such as Chrome, Firefox and Edge.
  • Weaknesses: some phone gallery apps, older TVs, car systems and editing apps do not open WebM files.
  • Best for: playing in a browser or in a flexible media player app.

If you ever end up with a WebM file that will not open in your gallery, a free media player such as VLC can usually play it.

MOV: Apple's QuickTime format

MOV is the QuickTime format created by Apple. iPhones and Mac computers often save camera recordings as MOV files, frequently using H.264 or HEVC inside. The MOV and MP4 formats are close relatives, so many apps handle both.

  • Strengths: works smoothly across Apple devices and in professional editing software.
  • Weaknesses: some Android phones, Windows apps and websites are less comfortable with MOV, especially when it contains HEVC.
  • Best for: recording and editing on Apple devices.

Does the format change quality?

Not by itself. Quality depends mostly on resolution, bitrate and the codec used, not the container name. A well-made WebM and a well-made MP4 at the same resolution can look almost identical. What changes quality is converting from one format to another: each time video is re-compressed, a little detail can be lost. If you already have a file that plays where you need it, there is no reason to convert it. Our guides on how compression works and resolution vs bitrate explain more.

When a video will not play

  • Try another player. A different media player app often handles formats or codecs that the built-in gallery does not.
  • Check the codec. Many player apps can show file information. If it says HEVC or AV1 and your device is older, that may be the cause.
  • Check the file finished downloading. A partly downloaded file may refuse to open or stop halfway. See why downloads stop.
  • Update your apps and system. Updates often add support for newer codecs.

What about audio files?

Audio has its own formats, such as MP3 and M4A. If you only need the sound from a video you are allowed to keep, saving audio alone makes a much smaller file. See MP3 vs M4A explained.

The short version

For videos you want to keep on a phone and share with others, MP4 with H.264 is the safest choice. WebM is great in browsers, and MOV is at home on Apple devices. If a file will not play, the problem is usually the codec or the app, not the video itself, and a different player often solves it.
