Battery tips for long downloads

Published October 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Saving a long video or a batch of files can take a while, and phones are built to save power whenever they can. That is good for battery life, but it can slow down or interrupt downloads at the worst moment. A few simple habits help you finish big downloads without draining your battery or losing progress.

What uses power during a download

  • The screen. For most phones, the display is the biggest single drain. Keeping it on at full brightness while you wait uses a lot of power.
  • The radio. Wi-Fi and mobile data both use energy, and mobile data uses noticeably more when the signal is weak because the phone works harder to stay connected.
  • Writing to storage. Saving the file uses a little power, though much less than the screen or radio.
  • Other apps. Apps running in the background, such as navigation, games or video calls, compete for both battery and bandwidth.

How battery saver affects downloads

Battery saver (Android) and Low Power Mode (iPhone) reduce what the phone does in the background. They may limit background network use, pause syncing, and put apps to sleep sooner. Many Android phones also have extra power management that stops apps the system thinks are not being used.

That can interrupt a download that is running in a browser tab you have left in the background. Downloads handled by the phone's built-in download manager, like those from the AllVid Android app, are usually more resilient, but very aggressive power settings can still slow them down.

Practical tips

1. Plug in for big jobs

If you are saving long videos or many files, connect a charger or power bank first. This is the simplest way to avoid both a flat battery and battery saver kicking in halfway through.

2. Use Wi-Fi with a good signal

A strong Wi-Fi connection is faster and usually more power efficient than mobile data with weak signal. Faster downloads also mean less total time with the radio working. See mobile data vs Wi-Fi.

3. Turn down the screen, or let it turn off when you can

If you are using AllVid in a browser, keep the tab open and the screen on until the download finishes, because browsers may pause background pages. Lower the brightness to save power while you wait. In the Android app, the phone's download manager carries on with the screen off, so you can lock your phone and let it work.

4. Pause battery saver temporarily

If battery saver is on and a download keeps stalling, turn it off for a few minutes while the file finishes, ideally while plugged in. Remember to turn it back on afterwards if you rely on it.

5. Do not start too many at once

Running many large downloads at the same time on a slow connection can make all of them take longer, keeping the phone busy for more time overall. AllVid's Settings let you choose how many downloads run at once; a lower number is often smoother on weak connections.

6. Close what you do not need

Close heavy apps such as games or video calls while a large download runs. They use power and bandwidth that the download could be using.

7. Choose a sensible size

A smaller file finishes sooner. If you will only watch on your phone, 720p usually looks great and downloads much faster than 1080p. See 360p vs 720p vs 1080p.

Looking after your battery long term

  • Avoid heat. Do not leave your phone in direct sun, on a car dashboard or under a pillow while charging and downloading. Heat is one of the main causes of battery wear.
  • Use a good charger and cable. Stick to the one that came with your phone or a reputable brand.
  • Do not worry about small top-ups. Modern lithium-ion batteries do not need to be fully drained. Many phones also offer an option to limit charging to around 80 to 85 percent to reduce wear; consider turning it on if you often charge overnight.
  • Keep software updated. Updates often include battery and power management improvements.

If a download stopped because of the battery

Charge your phone, reconnect to a stable network, and try the download again. Check that the half-finished file is not taking up space; leftover pieces may have names ending in .part, .tmp or .crdownload. Our guide on why downloads stop covers other causes too.

Watching what you downloaded

Once your videos are saved, playing them offline still uses power, mostly for the screen. Lower the brightness, use airplane mode when you do not need a connection, and bring a charger on long trips. Our guide to watching saved videos offline has more ideas.

The short version

Plug in, use good Wi-Fi, avoid battery saver during big downloads, and keep the browser tab open if you are using the website. Or use the Android app and let the phone's download manager do the work with the screen off.
